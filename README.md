# ONE — HUMAN-TO-REALITY ENGINE

> **Start from one.  
> Understand one person.  
> Solve one real problem.  
> Test it cheaply.  
> Learn from what happens.  
> Then build the next thing.**

---

## 0. WHAT IS THIS?

This is an imperfect README for a very large idea.

The idea is simple:

**People have problems.  
People have ideas.  
People have knowledge.  
People have different lives.  
Technology should meet them where they are.**

We want to build a system that helps a person move from:

**“I am thinking about this…”**

to:

**“I tested it.”**

to:

**“This is what the data says.”**

to:

**“Now I know what to build next.”**

We call this the:

# HUMAN-TO-REALITY ENGINE

It is not supposed to be one app forever.

It is a way of building.

---

# 1. WHY ARE WE STARTING SO SMALL?

Because very big ideas can lie.

A person can say:

> “I will change transportation.”

But they may never study one passenger.

A person can say:

> “I will change education.”

But they may never ask one student what is actually difficult.

A person can say:

> “I will build the future.”

But they may not know how reality behaves today.

So we start with:

# ONE.

One person.

One problem.

One journey.

One observation.

One prediction.

One experiment.

One result.

One lesson.

Then another.

### We are not shrinking the vision.

**We are shrinking the first experiment.**

---

# 2. WHY TRANSPORTATION FIRST?

Because transportation is everywhere.

A person can be:

- a student
- a market trader
- a bus driver
- a mechanic
- a nurse
- an engineer
- a teacher
- a founder
- an office worker
- unemployed
- wealthy
- poor

…and still need to move.

In Lagos, many people use the transport systems that already exist around them:

**danfo, korope, shared rides, bus stops, walking, informal route knowledge and local arrangements.**

Some people have smartphones.

Some have old phones.

Some use button phones.

Some do not use transport cards.

Some have data every day.

Some do not.

Some know technology very well.

Some know the road better than the technology knows the road.

### We want to learn from that reality.

Not pretend it does not exist.

---

# 3. THE FIRST PRODUCT

The first experiment is a small:

# LAGOS MOBILITY PREDICTION ENGINE

It asks:

> **What is this journey likely to be like today?**

Not only:

**“How much is the bus?”**

But eventually:

- What fare should I expect?
- How crowded may it be?
- How long might I wait?
- How difficult might the journey be?
- Is rain likely to change the journey?
- Is this time usually better or worse?
- What alternative may be easier?
- How confident are we?

The first version can be extremely simple.

The intelligence can become extremely deep later.

# 5. A VERY SIMPLE EXAMPLE

A passenger says:

> “I am going from Ifako to Yaba at 7 AM on Friday.”

The system may learn from previous observations:

- time
- day
- route
- crowd
- traffic
- weather
- observed fares
- waiting time
- travel time

Then it may say:

> **Expected journey:**  
> Fare: ₦X–₦Y  
> Crowd: High  
> Travel difficulty: High  
> Confidence: Medium

The numbers are only examples until real data is collected.

Then the passenger travels.

The passenger reports what really happened.

Now the system has:

**prediction → reality**

That difference matters.

---

# 6. WHY FAILURE IS ALLOWED

We want to make the first failures cheap.

A bad first prediction is not automatically a useless result.

It can become:

> “Why was the prediction wrong?”

That creates another question.

Another question creates another hypothesis.

Another hypothesis creates another experiment.

Another experiment creates another dataset.

So:

# FAILURE → INFORMATION → NEW HYPOTHESIS

We do not celebrate failure for its own sake.

We value **cheap learning**.

### Principle:

> **Minimize the cost of being wrong, so being wrong becomes useful.**

# 15. OUR CONSTITUTION

### Article 1 — Reality first
We separate what we believe from what we can demonstrate.

### Article 2 — Start small
We test the smallest useful version before spending heavily.

### Article 3 — Humans remain in control
The system recommends. People decide.

### Article 4 — Accessibility matters
A useful system should consider different devices, incomes, languages and abilities.

### Article 5 — Local knowledge matters
Knowledge does not become valuable only after it enters a laboratory.

### Article 6 — Evidence can change our mind
The system must be allowed to disagree with its creator.

### Article 7 — People can leave
No trapped users. No fake freedom.

### Article 8 — Failure is recorded
We do not hide failed experiments when they teach us something important.

### Article 9 — The constitution can evolve
Rules can change, but changes must be visible and explainable.

### Article 10 — Build for people who disagree with us
A system that only works for believers is not a strong system.
