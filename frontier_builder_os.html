01 / Core ideology

What this person is actually trying to do

Not “I have many interests.” The stronger interpretation is: “I am building a general method for converting knowledge into working systems.”

IDENTITY

Generalist → Integrator → Builder

Learn across domains, then connect the domains. Mathematics gives structure; engineering gives physical reality; computing gives scale; psychology gives human insight; neuroscience gives biological context; entrepreneurship gives economic survival.

METHOD

Hypothesis first

Every strong idea becomes a claim that can be tested. A test produces data. Data creates another hypothesis. The system keeps learning instead of pretending the first idea was perfect.

MATERIAL

Existing things are raw material

Scraps, open tools, old inventions, failed projects, forgotten principles and ordinary objects are not useless. The question is what new function can emerge when they are recombined.

COMMUNICATION

Make the invisible understandable

Deep thinking is not enough. The builder must create diagrams, language, simulations, books, prototypes, demonstrations and questions that allow another person to see the system.

INSTITUTION

Build rules that resist manipulation

Systems need constitutions, permissions, transparent incentives, audit trails, exit rights and adaptation mechanisms so that growth does not require blind trust in one person.

MISSION

Rebuild from first principles

The extreme thought experiment is useful: “If everything disappeared, what knowledge, infrastructure, skills and institutions would we rebuild first?” That turns patriotism or ambition into an engineering problem.

02 / Different people, different translation

The same person seen through six lenses

04 / Living constitution

Rules for a system that can grow without becoming a dictatorship

The constitution is not about controlling people. It is about creating predictable rules for adaptation, participation, accountability and exit.

FOUNDING PRINCIPLES
  1. Reality before reputation. Claims must eventually face evidence.
  2. Questions before certainty. A good question can be more valuable than a confident answer.
  3. Build small before scaling big. A prototype earns the right to become an institution.
  4. Every system needs an exit. People should be able to leave, fork, adapt or disagree without destroying the whole structure.
  5. Power requires constraints. No individual should be the only source of truth, permission or verification.
  6. Data should create learning. Measurement is for improving the system, not merely decorating presentations.
  7. Old knowledge is evidence, not a prison. Ancient principles can inspire new designs while remaining open to testing.
  8. Teach the method. The goal is not followers; it is people who can eventually build without you.
ANTI-CORRUPTION CHECKS

Any growing builder system should ask:

  • Who verifies the verifier?
  • Can the rules be changed secretly?
  • What happens when the founder is wrong?
  • Can participants see the assumptions behind decisions?
  • What incentive would cause someone to manipulate the system?
  • Can another team reproduce the result?
  • Can the system survive if the original builder disappears?

The ideal: a system that becomes stronger through criticism rather than weaker through disagreement.

06 / Communication layer

Questions that make the builder understandable

The fastest way to understand a complicated person is often to ask better questions.

Origin

  • What problem keeps returning to your mind?
  • What did you notice that other people ignored?
  • Which old principle do you think deserves another test?

Method

  • How do you decide whether an idea is worth testing?
  • What would prove you wrong?
  • What do you measure before you scale?

People

  • Who is the system designed to serve?
  • What would a poor person see differently from an investor?
  • Who can challenge you safely?

Technology

  • What should be software, hardware, data, education or infrastructure?
  • What existing tool can become raw material?
  • What must remain human?

Institution

  • What rule prevents manipulation?
  • How can people exit or fork the system?
  • How does the institution survive the founder?

Future

  • What would this create if it worked for 20 years?
  • Which prediction are you testing?
  • What new problems would success create?
07 / Network architecture

One person is not the final system

The long-term vision is a network of specialists who understand themselves, know what they can contribute and can connect into larger projects.

Researchers

Turn unknowns into evidence and expose weak assumptions.

Engineers

Convert principles into reliable physical and digital systems.

Computational builders

Model complexity, simulate outcomes and build scalable tools.

Designers & communicators

Make sophisticated ideas usable and understandable.

Entrepreneurs

Find real demand, distribution, incentives and sustainable economics.

Institution builders

Create governance, standards, memory, accountability and continuity.

The network principle

“I do not need to become every profession. I need to understand enough of many professions to connect the right people, identify the missing capability, build a first version, and create the conditions for others to become excellent.”

08 / The communication contract

How to explain this person without making them sound vague

Big ambitions become credible when the language connects them to specific behavior.

Do not saySay instead
“I want to change everything.”“I want to build small, testable systems whose successful patterns can be scaled.”
“I know I won't fail.”“I treat failure as unacceptable as a stopping point; when a method fails, I change the method and keep learning.”
“I have too many ideas.”“I maintain a large hypothesis space, then narrow it using evidence, constraints and experiments.”
“I want to build a new government.”“I am interested in designing institutional rules that are adaptable, auditable, difficult to manipulate and useful to the people inside them.”
“I like ancient knowledge and futuristic technology.”“I search for durable principles across history and test whether they can solve modern engineering and human problems.”
